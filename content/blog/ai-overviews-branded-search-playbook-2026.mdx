Google your own company name right now. Not your best keyword — your actual name. Do it on your phone, logged out.

Odds are you will see something you did not buy, did not write, and cannot edit: an AI-generated summary of your business sitting above your website. It describes what you do, where you work, maybe how much you charge — and it learned all of that from the public web, which means it can get it wrong with complete confidence.

This is not a future scenario. According to [DemandSphere's daily tracking](https://www.demandsphere.com/blog/branded-ai-overviews-september-2026/), AI Overviews appeared on just 26.12% of tracked branded keywords on 1 September 2026. By 27 September the share hit 90.48%, and it closed the month at 82.06%. A separate check by Nectiv co-founder Chris Long found AI Overviews on 93 of 100 enterprise brands tested. [Semrush's own coverage](https://www.semrush.com/blog/ai-overviews-appearing-for-major-brands/) confirms the same picture, with Ahrefs measuring 82.91% of US desktop branded keywords showing an Overview on 29 September.

Branded search used to be the one part of SEO you never had to think about. Someone hears your name, types it in, lands on your site. That assumption just broke, in the space of about three weeks.

This playbook covers what changed, what the Overview actually says about you, and the exact audit we run for clients — including the new Google rules from 1 October that make fake author profiles and unreviewed AI content a liability.

## Why branded search was the last safe room

Most SEO panic is about generic queries — "web development company", "best CRM for small business". Those battles were already messy. Branded search was different: the searcher wanted *you*. Google knew it, showed your site first, and the click was nearly guaranteed.

That guarantee quietly disappeared. Consider the difference between the old and new branded result:

| | Old branded result | New branded result (AI Overview) |
|---|---|---|
| Who writes the pitch | Your site's title and meta description | Google's AI, synthesised from many sources |
| First impression | Your homepage | A summary that may cite competitors, reviews, or stale listings |
| Accuracy | Fully in your control | Depends on every public source mentioning you |
| Click behaviour | Direct to your site | Searcher may get their answer without ever clicking |

The data makes the timing unmistakable. DemandSphere's tracker hovered at 26–35% through most of September, jumped to 57.43% on the 18th, and then tripled again in the final week. Google has not announced a change behind the increase. Whether it was a deliberate rollout or shifting AI systems, the effect is the same: your brand search is no longer just your listing.

The click impact is genuinely debated, and we will not pretend otherwise. [Seer Interactive found organic click-through 61% lower on queries with an AI Overview](https://soedja.com/blog/ai-overviews-branded-queries-september-2026), while Amsive measured an 18.7% click-through *lift* on branded keywords showing one. Our read: on branded queries, the Overview does not always steal the click — but it always shapes the first impression. If it pulls your hours from an outdated directory or your services from a bad review, you lose the visitor before your site loads.

## What the Overview actually says about you

Here is the uncomfortable part: Google's AI does not read your website's press release version of you. It synthesises a picture from everything public, weighted toward what appears most often and most recently. In practice, the sources that matter most are:

1. **Your Google Business Profile** — hours, services, service areas, photos. The single highest-leverage source for local and service businesses.
2. **Your About and Contact pages** — the canonical description of who you are and what you sell.
3. **Reviews** — Google reviews and third-party review sites get quoted or paraphrased more than founders expect.
4. **Directory listings** — Clutch, Yelp, Trustpilot, local directories. Stale entries here haunt you for years.
5. **Social profiles** — LinkedIn, Facebook, Instagram bios and posts.
6. **News and mentions** — press coverage, podcast appearances, anything indexed.

The failure mode is always the same: the facts disagree across sources, and the AI picks the wrong one. A studio we audited this month had its old address from a 2023 directory listing repeated in the Overview while its own site showed the new one. Nobody clicks through an AI answer that already gave them the wrong address — they just go somewhere else.

The fix has a clear order of operations, because not all sources carry equal weight. Fix the Business Profile first (it feeds the local panel and much of the Overview), then your own site, then directories, then reviews you can respond to.

## The 1 October rulebook: Google's new AI content and author guidance

The same week branded AI Overviews were peaking, Google tightened the rules on AI-generated content itself. On 1 October 2026, Google updated its generative AI content guidance to state that it is **critical to manually fact-check and review all AI-generated content** before publishing. [PPC Land's coverage](https://ppc.land/google-says-fake-author-profiles-signal-a-low-quality-page/) notes the review explicitly extends beyond body text to titles, meta descriptions, structured data and image alt text.

Its helpful-content documentation added something sharper: fabricated creator profiles — AI-generated headshots, invented names, false credentials used to make content look human-written — are now explicitly called a **form of deception**, and deception makes a page low quality. [TechWyse's breakdown](https://www.techwyse.com/news/search-seo/google-helpful-content-fake-authors-ai-quality-guidance) is worth reading for the full wording.

Our judgement call, after running content for clients through this for years: Google is not banning AI-assisted content. It is banning AI-assisted content that nobody checked. That distinction lands directly on branded search, because the AI Overview is increasingly what "your website" looks like to a first-time searcher — and if your own site is full of unreviewed AI text while a directory carries real reviews, guess which one the Overview trusts.

Three things to do this week, in order:

1. **Audit every byline on your site.** Every name, photo and credential must belong to a real person with provable expertise. If your blog's health columnist is a stock photo with a generated biography, replace the byline or remove the article. This is the explicit example Google warns against.
2. **Add a human review step to anything AI-assisted.** Not a grammar check — a fact check. Prices, dates, statistics, claims about your own services. If your studio uses AI to draft blog posts (we do, openly), a human verifies every concrete claim before publish.
3. **Check your metadata too.** AI-written title tags and meta descriptions can hallucinate offers you never made. Search Console's "HTML improvements" section and a quick site: search of your own titles will surface the worst cases.
## The spam update that finished this week

One more thing landed while all of this was happening. Google's September 2026 spam update — announced 24 September, aimed at sites violating spam policies — [completed its rollout on 8 October](https://www.seroundtable.com/google-september-2026-spam-update-done-42235.html), roughly two weeks on. Early analysis pointed at templated, mass-produced pages: the "service in [city]" page farms, programmatic content published at scale.

This matters for branded search diagnosis. If your branded clicks dropped in late September, you have two candidate explanations:

- **The spam update** — you would see drops across branded *and* non-branded queries, often sharp, sometimes 50%+ overnight.
- **The AI Overview rollout** — branded queries still rank fine in Search Console's average position, but clicks fall because the Overview satisfies the searcher above your listing.

Check this in Search Console before you touch anything. Performance report, filter to your brand queries, compare 20–23 September (before the volatility) against 9–11 October (after both events settled). If impressions held steady but clicks fell, that is an Overview interception pattern, not a penalty. We covered the same diagnostic approach in our [AI Mode traffic guide](/blog/google-ai-mode-traffic-drop-2026), and the logic transfers directly.

Do not rewrite your whole site in a panic either way. The spam update does periodic refreshes and Google says recovery can take months — so the highest-ROI move right now is the branded audit below, not a site rebuild.

## The brand-search audit: our exact process

This is the process we run for [our SEO clients](/services). Budget about two hours the first time, then twenty minutes a month to maintain.

### Step 1 — See what searchers see (15 minutes)

Search your brand name and your brand + city on a logged-out phone and on desktop. Record for each:

- Does an AI Overview appear? If yes, screenshot it.
- Every fact it states: services, hours, prices, locations, founding year.
- Every source it cites — click through and note which ones are accurate.

Do the same for your top three competitors. You will quickly see the pattern: whoever has the cleanest, most consistent public facts gets the best Overview.

### Step 2 — Fix the source of truth (30 minutes)

Your Google Business Profile is the single page you cannot afford to be stale. Update hours, services, service areas, photos and the business description. If you have multiple locations, each profile needs the same treatment — inconsistencies between your own locations get synthesised into nonsense.

Our earlier [Google Business Profile playbook](/blog/google-business-profile-optimization-2026) covers the full checklist; the AI Overview angle adds one priority: the profile's service descriptions are now quoted verbatim in AI answers, so write them the way you would explain the service to a customer, not as keyword soup.

### Step 3 — Make your site the canonical source (30 minutes)

The Overview prefers clear, quotable facts. Add or tighten these on your own site:

- An **About page** with a plain-English description of what you do, who you serve, and where. Not a mission statement — a description.
- A **Contact page** with current address, phone, hours, and service areas.
- An **FAQ page or section** that answers the exact questions the Overview got wrong. Google's guidance rewards accuracy and originality; an FAQ that directly contradicts a stale directory listing gives the AI something fresher to quote.
- **Structured data** — LocalBusiness or Organization schema with correct name, address, phone, hours, and sameAs links to your social profiles. Reviewed by a human, per the 1 October guidance.

### Step 4 — Clean the directories (30 minutes)

Search `"your company name"` in quotes and work through the first three pages. Claim, correct or suppress anything stale: old addresses, old phone numbers, services you no longer offer. In Pakistan, directories like local business listings and Facebook pages often outrank the company's own site for its brand name — so this step carries extra weight for businesses selling here.

![Auditing and verifying your business identity online](/blog/ai-overviews-branded-search-playbook-2026/fig2.webp)

### Step 5 — Answer the reviews (15 minutes)

You cannot delete most bad reviews, but you can outrank them in the AI's synthesis. Respond to every review from the last six months — factually, briefly, humanly. A thoughtful owner response attached to a critical review is exactly the kind of fresh, original, human-verified content the new guidance wants to see rewarded.

### Step 6 — Track it monthly

Add a recurring monthly check: the same brand queries, the same screenshots, one spreadsheet. AI Overview behaviour is volatile — DemandSphere's data jumped from 57% to 31% and back within days mid-September — so a single snapshot lies. A monthly series tells the truth.

## When to worry, and when not to

Not every AI Overview is a problem. Here is our honest triage:

**Do not panic if:** the Overview appears, states your facts correctly, and cites your site or Business Profile. That is arguably better than a blue link — it pre-sells you before the click.

**Fix this month if:** the Overview states wrong facts, cites competitors for your branded query, or your clicks fell while impressions held steady.

**Call someone if:** branded clicks collapsed across the board at the same time as non-branded traffic — that smells like the spam update or a manual action, and the playbook is different (start with the Manual Actions report in Search Console, then review against the spam policies).

One more thing to know: Google's September guidance made it explicit that scaled, unreviewed content is a spam risk, and the 1 October update extended the human-review requirement to metadata. If your marketing team publishes five AI-drafted posts a week with no fact-check, the branded-search risk is the least of your worries — but it is the most visible one, because it shows up on your own name.

## Google is selling the space around your brand's AI answer

The AI Overview is not just an editorial problem. It is now advertising inventory. Google has confirmed that ads can appear above and below AI Overviews in more than 200 countries — which means a competitor can pay to sit directly above the AI summary of *your* business, on *your* brand query.

For anyone running paid search, this changes the maths on branded terms. Two developments from the last fortnight are directly relevant:

**AI Max is now the default gravity.** Google's AI Max for Search — which lets its AI generate ad copy and match to longer, conversational queries — was adopted by half a million advertisers in its first year and is now Google's fastest-growing search ads product. Google says advertisers using it see 27% more conversions at a similar cost. The migration from legacy automatically-created assets largely completed in September 2026, so if you have not checked what Google's AI is writing about your brand lately, do it this week — and preview the generated samples before Q4 spend ramps up.

**Search queries got longer.** Dan Taylor, Google's VP of Global Ads, noted at DMEXCO 2026 that searches in AI Mode run three times longer than traditional searches, and one in six is made by voice or image. That matters for branded search too: "AISquadX web design prices" is a very different query from "web design", and the AI Overview on the longer version is more likely to quote specific facts — your prices, your competitors' prices — from wherever it finds them.

Our position is blunt: if your brand query shows an AI Overview and you are not bidding on your own brand terms, you are donating the most valuable slot on the page. Branded clicks are the cheapest clicks you will ever buy, and the AI Overview just made them competitive. A small defensive branded campaign — exact match on your name, your product names, and common misspellings — costs most businesses under $100 a month and guarantees at least one asset on the page that you fully control.

What not to do: do not panic-bid on every AI Overview. Bid where you can see it — search your brand, check whether competitor ads appear above or below the Overview, and defend only the queries that show real commercial intent. Everything else is an organic problem, fixed by the audit above.

## Measuring branded AI visibility: the new reports

You cannot manage what you do not measure, and branded search measurement just got both harder and better. Harder, because the Overview sits between your impression and your click. Better, because Google has started shipping reports that show what is happening on AI surfaces.

Google's October 2026 updates included globally released **AI performance reports and controls** for Search, plus a new multimodal search filter in the Performance reports. If you run Merchant Center, there are now **AI Performance Insights** showing your share of voice against competitors directly on AI Mode and AI Overviews — the first time Google has let brands see their visibility inside AI answers rather than guessing.

Here is the measurement stack we recommend for a business that wants honest numbers:

1. **Search Console, branded query segment** — impressions, clicks, CTR and average position for queries containing your brand name. Watch the gap between impressions (which the Overview does not affect) and clicks (which it does).
2. **AI performance reports** — your visibility on AI surfaces specifically, once available in your account. Compare month over month, not day to day; the volatility is real.
3. **Manual screenshots, monthly** — no tool yet shows you exactly what the Overview *said*. A dated screenshot of your top five brand queries is the cheapest competitive intelligence you own.
4. **CRM source tracking** — ask new enquiries how they found you. If "I Googled you" stays steady while branded clicks fall, the Overview is doing your selling for you. If enquiries fall too, it is doing someone else's.

One trap to avoid: rank trackers. [Ranktracker published an investigation on 7 October](https://www.einnews.com/pr_news/948157260/ranktracker-restores-google-top-100-rankings-as-seo-platforms-face-widespread-disruptions) showing that several SEO platforms were returning incomplete SERPs — sometimes only one to three results — and reporting keywords as "lost" when the data collection was actually at fault. If your tracker shows a branded keyword collapsing this month, verify it manually before you act. The tool might be broken; the ranking might be fine.

## The Pakistan and US playbooks differ — here is how

Our clients split roughly between the US and Pakistan, and the branded-search audit is not identical in both markets. The principles are the same; the battlefield is not.

**In Pakistan**, branded search is disproportionately decided off your website. Facebook pages, WhatsApp Business listings, and local directories frequently outrank a company's own domain for its brand name — sometimes the Facebook page *is* the de facto homepage. The AI Overview draws heavily from these. The practical consequence: your Facebook About section and WhatsApp Business profile need the same care as your website's About page, because to Google's AI they are equally authoritative sources about you. Urdu and Roman Urdu variants of your brand name matter too — searchers type both, and the Overview can differ between them. Check both spellings.

**In the US**, the directory layer is deeper and more formalised: Yelp, the Better Business Bureau, industry-specific directories like Clutch or Houzz depending on your trade. Reviews carry more weight in the AI's synthesis, and American searchers are more likely to add modifiers ("reviews", "pricing", "vs") that trigger longer, more detailed Overviews. The fix is the same six-step audit, but budget more time for the review-response and directory-cleanup steps — a US service business typically has three to five times as many listings to reconcile as a Pakistani one.

Both markets share the same blind spot: founders check their brand search on their own phone, logged in, in their own city. Google personalises. The logged-out check on a different device — or better, a colleague's phone in another city — shows you what strangers actually see. That is the version the AI Overview is optimised for, and it is the only one that matters.


## FAQ

**What are AI Overviews on branded searches?**

When someone searches your company or product name, Google now often shows an AI-generated summary of your business at the top of the results, before your own website. DemandSphere data shows this happened on about 82% of tracked branded queries by the end of September 2026, up from roughly 26% at the start of the month.

**Do AI Overviews hurt branded traffic?**

The honest answer: it depends, and the data is mixed. Seer Interactive found organic click-through 61% lower on queries showing an AI Overview, while Amsive measured an 18.7% click-through lift on branded keywords with one. What is not in dispute is that the AI summary now shapes the first impression before your site gets a chance.

**Where does Google's AI Overview get its facts about my business?**

From the same public sources a stranger would use: your Google Business Profile, your About and Contact pages, reviews on Google and third-party sites, directory listings, and social profiles. If any of those are stale or wrong, the Overview can repeat the stale facts as confident answers.

**How do I fix an AI Overview that gets my business wrong?**

Fix the underlying sources, not the Overview itself — there is no edit button. Update your Google Business Profile, correct your About and Contact pages, clean up directory listings, and answer the wrong facts directly in an FAQ on your site. Then recheck the query after a few days to confirm the summary updated.

**What did Google change on 1 October 2026 about AI content?**

Google updated its guidance to state it is critical to manually fact-check all AI-generated content before publishing, including titles, meta descriptions, structured data and image alt text. Its helpful-content guidance now explicitly calls fabricated creator profiles — AI-generated headshots, invented names, false credentials — a form of deception that makes a page low quality.

**Is my branded traffic drop caused by the September 2026 spam update?**

Possibly, if you lost rankings across branded and non-branded queries at the same time. The September 2026 spam update ran from 24 September to 8 October 2026 and hit templated, mass-produced pages. Check Search Console's Performance report, comparing branded clicks from 20–23 September against 9–11 October. If branded queries are steady but clicks fell, the likely culprit is the AI Overview intercepting the click, not a penalty.
