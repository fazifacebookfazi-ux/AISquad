Your traffic report for August says the same thing ours did last month: rankings fine, clicks down. You check Search Console. Impressions are steady — arguably up. But the click-through rate looks like someone took a bite out of it, and the sessions never made it to GA4.

Nothing is broken. That's the hard part. Nothing is broken, and the clicks are still gone.

They were intercepted. In June 2026, Google showed an AI Overview on 39.4% of US desktop searches, up from 25.8% a year earlier, according to [Comscore's Q2 2026 AI Intelligence Report](https://www.b2the7.com/news-blog/marketing-trends-september-28-2026). When AI Mode became the default for 1,100 real Chrome users, the share of searches leading to an outside website dropped by 18.8 percentage points.

Read that again. Not 18.8 percent of their traffic. 18.8 *percentage points*. For publishers and businesses living on search, that is the difference between a quarter that's tight and a quarter that's terrifying.

## The September 2026 study: what it actually proved

The paper is titled *AI in Search Reduces Publisher Referrals Without Improving User Experience: Experimental Evidence*, from researchers at the University of Pennsylvania and Northeastern University. [Search Engine Land covered it on 23 September](https://searchengineland.com/google-ai-mode-cuts-clicks-satisfaction-study-490494), and [Search Engine Journal published a detailed methodology writeup](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/research-shows-google-ai-mode-sends-less-clicks-is-a-poor-user-experience/590221/) the same week. It's worth understanding because it's rare: a randomised field experiment on real browsing behaviour, not a survey, not a model, not an agency's press release.

Here's what they did. They enrolled 1,444 people and tracked 1,100 of them for seven days through a browser extension that recorded searches, clicks and result-page snapshots. Then they randomised the participants into three groups: normal Google, a version with AI Overviews hidden and AI Mode blocked, and a version that forced searches into AI Mode. Three days of normal behaviour first, then seven days of the assigned experience, then a post-experiment survey completed by 956 people.

### The numbers nobody should ignore

- **Outside clicks fell 18.8 percentage points** when AI Mode became the default. Not a rounding error. Nearly one in five searches that used to leave Google now stays inside it.
- **AI Overviews alone were brutal.** A companion arm of the experiment showed outbound organic clicks fell 39.8% when an AI Overview appeared, and zero-click searches rose 34.5%. Removing the overview pushed clicks from 0.37 to 0.62 per search — consistent with the earlier 2026 Agarwal & Sen field finding of a 38% causal reduction in clicks.
- **People searched less.** The AI Mode group ran almost one fewer Google search session per day. Heavy users — people averaging more than four sessions daily — cut about two sessions a day.
- **Clicks to news sites fell.** Clicks to Reddit fell by 21.2 points. Reddit, the internet's beloved human forum, lost a fifth of its search referral share in the AI Mode group.

Now, fair caveats — because we're not going to oversell this. The study *forced* AI Mode on people. Before the experiment, AI Mode accounted for just 0.6% of these users' searches; 94.7% of the AI Mode group's searches were routed there by the researchers, not by choice. The sample skewed younger and highly educated. It lasted seven days. This measures what happens when AI Mode *effectively becomes* the default, not what happens when searchers voluntarily choose it.

Still. Direction beats precision here. If Google keeps pushing AI Mode forward — and it keeps pushing, month after month — the click you used to count on gets rarer. Plan for the direction, not the exact number.

### The twist nobody expected: users didn't even like it

This is the part Google's public statements don't prepare you for. The AI Mode group reported *lower* satisfaction, lower perceived usefulness, less sense of control, and lower personalisation than people using normal Google. Trust in Google declined. About 34% gave negative feedback against 29% positive; common complaints were less control, difficulty navigating directly to websites, and limited links or source diversity.

And the cherry on top: the share of participants who wandered off to Bing, DuckDuckGo or Yahoo went up by 11.2 percentage points. Fewer clicks for websites, and no clear win for the searcher either. Google's aggressive AI push is, in the words of one summary, keeping people inside Google's walls longer — while making them less happy about being there.

Why does this matter for your business? Because it kills the comforting theory. The comforting theory was: "AI answers are fine, users love them, Google will route the motivated buyers to us anyway." The data says users don't even prefer the AI experience, *and* it still intercepts your traffic. You cannot wait for a user backlash to save your click-through rate. The intercept happens whether users enjoy it or not.

## The citation credit gap: being the source is not the same as being cited

Here's the finding from the Comscore report that should change your strategy more than any single number. Comscore studied who AI answers actually draw on — and being a source and being *cited* turned out to be two different things.

Tripadvisor was a source behind **61%** of the lodging answers Comscore studied. It earned a visible citation in only **21%**. The AI read Tripadvisor's data, used it, and left the link off most of the time.

Let that sink in. The dominant playbook of the last decade — publish the best content, earn the rankings, collect the traffic — assumed that being the best source meant being visited. In AI search, the chain breaks at the last link. You can do everything right and still watch the credit go to "Google AI" instead of your brand.

The report had a second pattern worth your attention: **YouTube and Reddit are two of the most cited sources in AI answers.** For retail searches, YouTube showed up among AI Overview citations 23% of the time, ahead of Amazon at 14% and Reddit at 12%. AI systems clearly weight first-hand, human, video and community content. That's also why our own studio blog now ships original numbers and screenshots rather than rewrites of other people's articles — machines can smell generic content, and so can the users.

And a third: the assistants are spreading out. ChatGPT still handled about half of AI prompt volume in June, down from 70% in January. Gemini climbed to 30% and Claude to 11%. Your customers are no longer all in one AI tool, so showing up in one of them isn't a plan anymore. Test your brand's visibility in ChatGPT, Gemini and Perplexity directly — type the questions your customers actually ask and see who gets named.

So the practical takeaway from this whole section: **your new KPI is being named, not being clicked.** Mentions inside the answer. Citations with your brand on them. Entity presence across the AI tools your buyers use. We'll get to tactics shortly. First, you need to see your own exposure clearly.

## Step one: diagnose your exposure in Search Console

Until this summer, most teams were guessing. Then Google shipped the one number we'd all been asking for. On **31 August 2026**, the Generative AI performance report reached every Search Console property worldwide — first [announced on the Search Central blog in June](https://netcontentseo.com/article/google-gives-publishers-a-dedicated-ai-visibility-report-in-search-console-search-and-discover-are-finally-separated-999), then rolled out in full.

Open Search Console, go to Performance, and find the generative AI report under Search. It shows how often your pages appeared inside AI Overviews and AI Mode, broken down by page, country, device and date. It is the first first-party number most teams have ever had for AI visibility on Google, and you can read it in two minutes.

But read it the way Google wrote it, because the fine print decides how you use it:

- **Impressions only.** No clicks, no click-through rate, no position. Google says more metrics will come "over time." Until then, you know how often you *appeared*, not how often it mattered.
- **No query dimension.** You can see *that* a page appeared in AI answers, not *what anyone asked* to summon it. That's the single biggest hole in the report.
- **AI Overviews and AI Mode are combined** into one number, though an appearance in each is a different event.
- **The data was in your totals all along.** The report is a filtered view of your existing Web search-type data, not new traffic on top. Never add it to your totals.
- **No API, no BigQuery export** as of September 2026 — manual CSV download only, and it covers Google only, not ChatGPT or Claude.

The practical way to use it: export the CSV monthly, sort by page, and look for pages with high AI impressions and falling clicks. That page is appearing but not converting the appearance — your prime candidate for the content work in the next section. Then check the country breakdown. A lot of our Pakistan-market clients are surprised here: AI Overview coverage on English queries is high even in South Asia, and if you're selling services to the US from Lahore or Karachi, the US slice of your AI impressions is the one that actually pays.

One more thing while you're in there: you'll see the new Search Generative AI opt-out control under Settings. Leave it on. We'll explain why in the "what not to do" section — but the short version is that opting out deletes you from the answer entirely, and an impression you can fight for beats no impression at all.

## Step two: chase mentions, not rankings

Here's the strategic shift, and it's the whole article in one sentence: **stop optimising only for the click; optimise to be the source the answer quotes.**

Rankings still matter — they feed the systems that pick citations — but they're the qualifier now, not the prize. The prize is your brand name appearing inside the answer. A mention can still send a customer your way: the person reads the answer, remembers the name, and types it into Google later, or walks into the AI tool and asks for you directly. That traffic won't show up as "organic search referral" in your analytics. It'll look like direct or brand search. Measure it that way instead.

What makes AI systems cite you, in practice? Our studio's [SEO for AI search playbook](/blog/seo-for-ai-search-2026) goes deep on this; the short version is fact-dense, quotable writing plus machine-readable business facts:

- **Write answers an AI can defend.** "We charge $25–$60 per hour" beats "our pricing is competitive." "Next.js 15 with Vercel" beats "modern tech stack." Every vague sentence is a sentence an AI can't quote. Go back through your money pages and replace adjectives with numbers.
- **One canonical fact sheet.** Your About page should read like a database entry: legal name, founded date, cities, team size, services with one-line definitions. AI systems cross-reference facts across the web. When your About says Lahore + Austin, your [Google Business Profile](/blog/seo-services-cost-pakistan-2026) says Lahore, and a directory says something else, confidence drops and the citation goes to a competitor whose facts agree with themselves.
- **Publish what can't be copied.** Original price ranges, build timelines, failure stories, benchmark numbers from your own work. The b2the7 analysis of this week's trends put it well: generic AI-written posts are being reported as slop by the million on LinkedIn. Your advantage is everything only you could have written.
- **Structure for extraction.** Clear headings, tables, short definition paragraphs. Not for Google's crawler — it stopped being about the crawler years ago — for the citation engine that skims your page in milliseconds deciding whether to quote you.

And test it directly. Ask ChatGPT, Gemini and Perplexity the ten questions your prospects ask most. Note who gets named. If it's not you, that's your content backlog — not your keyword backlog.

## Step three: feed the channels AI answers actually cite

Remember the Comscore citation data: YouTube at 23% for retail answers, Reddit at 12%. AI systems are hungry for human, first-hand content — and right now they're finding it in places most businesses ignore.

**YouTube is the highest-leverage move.** A 3-minute video answering one customer question does three jobs: it can be cited by AI answers, it ranks in its own right, and it's the format Google's video and AI surfaces both prefer. You don't need a production crew. We shoot ours with a phone, a lapel mic that cost about ₨8,000 (roughly $28), and a window. The trick is answering a *searched* question in the first 20 seconds: "How much does a Shopify store cost in Pakistan in 2026?" not "Welcome back to our channel." One useful video a week for six months beats a "content strategy" deck every time.

**Reddit and communities are the second.** AI answers cite Reddit because it reads like real people solving real problems. That means your presence there has to *be* real people solving real problems. No pitching. Answer questions in the subreddits your buyers read, link only when the link is genuinely the best answer, and accept that the payoff is indirect — a citation in an AI answer next quarter, not traffic today. We've seen clients' community answers start appearing in AI citations within 60–90 days of consistent, non-salesy participation.

**Your own site stays the hub**, but calibrate expectations: it's the source the AI reads, and the place the motivated buyer eventually lands. Everything else is distribution.

## Step four: convert the traffic you still get — harder

When fewer people arrive, each arrival has to count more. This is the unsexy part of the playbook and the part most articles skip: if AI Mode cut your organic sessions by 20% and you lift your conversion rate by 25%, you're ahead. Businesses obsess over traffic and ignore the leak in the bucket.

The five pages to fix this week:

1. **Homepage hero.** One sentence saying what you do and for whom, one visible contact path. No sliders, no "welcome to our website."
2. **Pricing page (or pricing section).** If you hide pricing because "it depends," you're donating leads to competitors who publish ranges. A range qualifies buyers; secrecy just scares them off.
3. **Contact page.** For Pakistan-market services, a WhatsApp button converts dramatically better than a form — we routinely see it outperform forms 3-to-1 on service pages. For US buyers, add a calendar link. Forms are the fallback, not the front door.
4. **Your top two service pages.** Add one testimonial with a name, one specific result with a number, and a single clear next step. Generic "we deliver excellence" copy is invisible to buyers and AI alike.
5. **Mobile experience.** Over 60% of our clients' traffic in Pakistan is mobile; if your contact button isn't thumb-reachable in the first screen, fix that before you spend a rupee on ads.

None of this requires a redesign. These are afternoon fixes, and we've measured the difference: a service page with published price ranges, a named testimonial and a WhatsApp CTA typically doubles enquiry rate versus the generic version it replaces.

## Step five: build channels no AI update can confiscate

This is the part of the strategy that is genuinely future-proof. Every channel you rent — Google rankings, Facebook reach, LinkedIn followers — has a landlord who can change the terms. This month's [weekly trends roundup](https://www.b2the7.com/news-blog/marketing-trends-september-28-2026) was a masterclass in landlord behaviour: Facebook capping link posts for some Pages unless they pay for Meta One, Google switching on AI checkout for Shopify stores *by default*. The platforms are not evil. They're just businesses, and you're the inventory.

The channels you own outright:

- **Email list.** Boring, old, and still the highest-ROI channel in marketing. A monthly email with one genuinely useful insight beats a weekly "newsletter" nobody opens. Start with a simple incentive: a pricing guide, a checklist, a template — something your buyers would save.
- **WhatsApp broadcast (for Pakistan and similar markets).** Open rates that email can only dream of. Keep it opt-in, keep it infrequent, keep every message useful. One message a week maximum, or people mute you and you've burned the channel.
- **YouTube subscribers.** YouTube is technically rented, but it's the rented channel least likely to be confiscated — it feeds Google's own AI answers, so Google has an incentive to keep creators happy.
- **Direct relationships.** The agency owners we know who are calmest about AI Mode are the ones whose pipeline is 60% referrals. No algorithm stands between you and a past client who sends you their friend.

The target mix we'd prescribe to a client today: no more than 40% of new business dependent on Google search. If you're at 80%, that's your real risk — bigger than any single algorithm update.

## What not to do

Three traps we're watching clients walk into this month:

**1. Don't opt out of Google's AI features.** Search Console now has a Search Generative AI toggle under Settings, defaulted to include. Sites that opt out get *no* traffic or impressions from AI Overviews and AI Mode — Google's own reporting confirms it. Yes, the click share inside an AI answer is small (Pew measured ~1% of visits clicking a cited source). But zero of zero is worse. Opting out is amputating the foot because the shoe pinches. The only businesses that should consider it are ones whose product *is* the information — publishers with paywalls, data vendors. For everyone else: stay in.

**2. Don't panic-rewrite your whole site.** AI search rewards specific, consistent, quotable content — which is the same thing good SEO always rewarded. A measured programme (ten pages this month, ten next) beats a three-week rewrite marathon that tanks your current rankings. Google's September 2026 spam update is still rolling out — a global two-week rollout targeting scaled content abuse — and a site-wide content churn in the middle of it is exactly how you get misdiagnosed as a churn-and-burn operation. Calm, sequential, measured.

**3. Don't buy your way out of a strategy problem.** Ads are a legitimate accelerant — but buying traffic to a site with a broken conversion path is just renting the same problem at a higher price. Fix the five pages first. Then ads. In that order.

## FAQ

### Why is my website traffic dropping in 2026 even though my rankings are stable?

Your rankings can hold while your clicks fall, because Google's AI answers now intercept the visit. Comscore's Q2 2026 data showed AI Overviews on 39.4% of US desktop searches, and a University of Pennsylvania / Northeastern field study found that when AI Mode became the default, the share of searches leading to an outside website dropped by 18.8 percentage points. The search happens; the trip to your site just never starts.

### What is the Google Search Console AI visibility report and where do I find it?

It's the Generative AI performance report Google rolled out to every property worldwide on 31 August 2026. In Search Console go to Performance and open the generative AI report under Search. It shows how often your pages appeared inside AI Overviews and AI Mode, broken down by page, country, device and date. The catch: it reports impressions only — no clicks, no queries, and no split between AI Overviews and AI Mode. [Google's help page](https://support.google.com/webmasters/answer/16984139) confirms it's drawn from your existing Web search data, not new traffic on top.

### Should I opt out of Google's AI features to protect my traffic?

Almost certainly not. Google's Search Generative AI opt-out control removes your pages from AI Overviews and AI Mode entirely, which means zero impressions and zero chance of being cited — you trade a smaller click share for none at all. For almost every local and service business, disappearing from the answer is worse than appearing in it.

### How do I get my business cited in Google AI Overviews?

Publish precise, quotable facts that an AI can defend: specific prices, named tools, step-by-step processes, original data. Keep your business facts consistent everywhere — your [About page](/about), Google Business Profile and directories must agree. Earn presence on the channels AI answers cite most, especially YouTube and Reddit. And tie your brand name tightly to every fact so the credit follows the content instead of going to a competitor who repeated it.

### What should small businesses do about zero-click searches?

Stop treating the search click as the product. Measure leads and calls, not sessions. Push the traffic you do get harder — tighten conversion on your top five pages. Build channels you own outright: an email list, a WhatsApp broadcast, a YouTube channel. And shift your SEO target from "rank first" to "be the named source inside the answer." One mention inside an AI answer can still send a customer your way even when nobody clicks through.

### Is AI Mode replacing Google search?

Not yet, but the direction is set. Before the September 2026 experiment, AI Mode accounted for just 0.6% of the test users' searches — almost nobody chose it voluntarily. The study shows what happens if Google pushes it harder: fewer outside clicks, one fewer search session per day, and 11.2 points more users drifting to Bing, DuckDuckGo or Yahoo. Plan for a slow bleed of clicks, not an overnight switch.
