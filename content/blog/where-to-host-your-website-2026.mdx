Your hosting provider quietly changed the price of your website last week, and if you're on Vercel you have until October 23 to decide what to do about it. On October 9, Vercel posted a changelog entry that's easy to skim past: deployment storage billing begins for existing Pro teams — $0.10 per GB-month for Deployment Storage and Functions Storage, with the deployment retention window changing to 30 days. Deployments older than 30 days start being deleted on October 23 unless you opt out in your retention settings first.

That line — deleted — is what makes this more than a routine price tweak. A deleted deployment can't be rolled back to. If you ever needed to revert production to a build from last spring for a client audit or a slow-moving release, that safety net now has an expiry date and a meter on it.

We've hosted client sites on all three of the big frontend platforms, plus Railway, Render and plain VPS boxes, and this latest Vercel move is a good excuse to answer a question business owners ask us constantly: where should I actually host my website in 2026? Not the marketing answer. The one based on who bills you for what, where the money leaks, and which platform fits which kind of site. Let's start with what changed, then compare your real options.

## What Vercel actually changed on October 9

Two things happened at once, and they're easy to confuse. Here's the clean version, taken from Vercel's own changelog:

1. **New paid storage meter.** Deployment Storage (your old deployments) and Functions Storage (your serverless function bundles) are now billed at $0.10 per GB-month for all Pro teams, [per Vercel's own changelog](https://vercel.com/changelog/deployment-storage-pricing-expands-to-existing-teams). Existing Pro teams had been outside this meter until now; billing start dates vary by team, and Vercel said it's emailing each team its date.

2. **Retention window shrinks to 30 days.** The deployment retention window changes to 30 days, and deployments older than 30 days are deleted starting October 23 unless you opt out in retention settings beforehand. A [companion changelog entry](https://vercel.com/changelog/new-pro-teams-now-default-to-30-day-deployment-retention) sets the same 30-day default for new Pro teams, and [independent coverage](https://firerun.io/vercel-pro-deployment-storage-billing-retention-2026/) confirms the rollout details. Teams already set to 30 days or shorter are unaffected. Deleted deployments cannot be restored or used for rollback.

You control the retention through a Deployment Retention Policy with separate periods for pre-production, production, canceled and errored deployments. The Usage page shows Deployment Storage and Functions Storage per project, so you can see which project is drawing the meter before the bill arrives.

This follows two earlier moves. On September 16, Vercel said Hobby teams over the 10 GB cap would lose deployments immediately, pointing them at Pro's $0.10/GB rate. And in September 2026, Flat Rate CDN went generally available for Pro teams — on by default for new teams, replacing the metered bandwidth line with a fixed tier and spike protection, with older teams able to switch over in billing settings.

The pattern is worth naming plainly: Vercel is gradually shifting from "deploy anything, pay mostly for traffic" to "every resource gets its own meter." None of the individual meters are expensive. The trap is that nobody reads a changelog email, nobody checks the Usage page, and the costs stack quietly.

## Why Vercel blamed coding agents — and they're right

The most interesting sentence in the whole announcement isn't about pricing. It's Vercel's stated reason for the change: coding agents have accelerated development, increasing deployment frequency and the amount of stored deployment history.

If you've let Claude Code, Cursor or any agent open a pull request for every small task, every one of those branches has been producing a preview deployment, and all of that output has been sitting in storage. A year ago, a team deployed a few times a week. Now an agent can generate dozens of preview deployments in a weekend while you sleep. Multiply that across every Vercel team on Earth and you get a storage bill Vercel can no longer absorb for free.

For most teams the actual dollar amounts will be small — at $0.10/GB-month you'd need 100 GB of stored deployments to pay $10. The real cost is the retention change, not the meter. But the deeper lesson applies wherever you host: your AI workflow changed your infrastructure profile, and the platforms are now pricing for the world agents created. Set spend limits and retention policies the same week you adopt an agent workflow, not six months later.

## The real question: what is your host billing you for?

Before comparing platforms, you need the mental model. The three big platforms charge for completely different things, and the cheapest choice depends entirely on your traffic and deploy pattern:

- **Vercel charges for bandwidth by the GB** (plus, now, storage, seats and a growing list of add-ons). Pick Vercel on the free tier alone and a viral post can surprise you.
- **Cloudflare Pages charges for builds and leaves bandwidth free.** Unlimited bandwidth on the free plan; 500 builds per month. Your cost comes from how often you deploy, not how popular you get.
- **Netlify charges a pool of credits** that bandwidth, builds and requests all draw from (free plan caps at 300 credits; Pro is 3,000).

That single difference is the whole decision for most sites. A brochure site with 500,000 monthly visitors costs nearly nothing on Cloudflare Pages and a real bill on Vercel. A site that deploys 40 times a day but serves little traffic is cheap on Vercel and expensive on Cloudflare. Know which curve you're on before you pick.

**Who Vercel is for now:** Next.js apps, teams that deploy constantly, businesses that value preview environments and framework integration more than the lowest bill. **Who should leave:** high-bandwidth static sites, anyone on Pro paying per-seat for developers who rarely deploy.

## Cloudflare Pages: the unlimited-bandwidth challenger

Cloudflare Pages is the platform the other two are losing momentum to on price, and the value proposition is brutally simple: unlimited free bandwidth, free commercial use, 500 free builds a month, and enterprise-grade DDoS protection that the others charge for. Its free tier permits commercial use — Vercel's Hobby plan strictly prohibits it.

The network behind it is the largest of the three (200+ edge locations, [per an October 2026 platform comparison](https://www.cloudpicked.com/cloudflare-pages-review-en.html)), and cold starts are effectively zero because compute runs on V8 isolates rather than containers. If your site is mostly static with some dynamic edge functions — a marketing site, a blog, a documentation portal, a landing-page-heavy SaaS — it's genuinely hard to justify paying anyone else.

The constraints are real though, and they're the kind that bite at month three, not day one:

- **Workers is not Node.js.** Some npm packages simply won't run. If your app depends on Node-specific APIs, you'll find out the hard way. Check compatibility before you migrate, not during.
- **The data story is Cloudflare-flavoured.** D1 (SQL), KV, R2 and Vectorize are good, but they're a different ecosystem from the Postgres-Neon-Supabase world. Greenfield projects adapt fine; existing apps with a relational database face real migration work.
- **Next.js support is good, not native.** Vercel will always ship Next.js features first. On Pages, the fancy new App Router features arrive later and occasionally with quirks.

**Our studio's take:** for static and edge-heavy sites with real traffic, Cloudflare Pages is the best value in hosting right now. We recommend it for content sites and marketing sites without hesitation. For complex Next.js apps with heavy server components, we stay on Vercel.

## Netlify: the quiet workhorse after the credit change

Netlify invented this category and still does the fundamentals well: build hooks, forms handling without a backend, split testing, identity. It's the most framework-agnostic of the three — Vercel is visibly Next.js-first, while Netlify treats every framework the same.

The big change is billing. Since April 2026, Netlify moved to credits: Pro is a flat $20/month with unlimited seats and 3,000 credits, Personal is $9/month for 1,000 credits, and the free plan caps at 300. The per-seat model is gone. For a team of five deploying a marketing site with forms, that's a meaningful saving versus Vercel's per-seat pricing.

Netlify's honest weakness is momentum: it's losing on price to Cloudflare and on framework integration to Vercel. But for a marketing site with forms — the most common business website in existence — it's a genuinely low-fuss choice, and the flat pricing with unlimited seats makes it the cheapest of the three for small teams on paper.

**Who Netlify is for:** small teams that want predictable billing, marketing sites, multi-framework shops. If your agency runs ten small client sites, Netlify's per-project pricing shape beats Vercel's per-seat model.

## The costs nobody puts in the comparison table

Comparison tables show you the headline prices. The money actually leaks somewhere else. These are the line items our studio checks on every hosting engagement, because we've been bitten by every one of them:

**Data egress to your own database.** If your database sits in one region and your functions run in another, you pay for every query twice over — once in latency, once in cash. One recent analysis of Vercel invoices found that placing functions far from the database raised compute costs by as much as 44% while adding a round trip to every request. Put your functions next to your data. This is a five-minute fix in project settings that most teams never make.

**Build minutes.** Nobody budgets for these because nobody watches them. Every agent-driven preview deployment burns build minutes (or Cloudflare build credits, or Netlify credits). A monorepo with a careless build config can run ten-minute builds fifty times a day. Cache your dependencies, split your projects, and stop building the entire app when only the blog changed.

**TLS certificates.** Boring, invisible, and about to matter more. Let's Encrypt is moving to 64-day certificate lifetimes starting February 2027, which turns manual renewal from a chore into a liability. If you're hand-rolling a VPS, your certificate automation needs to be bulletproof before that deadline — managed platforms handle this for you, and it's one of the quietest reasons they're worth paying for.

**Support tiers.** When your site is down on a Friday night, the difference between a platform's free community support and a paid tier with real humans is the difference between a weekend of panic and a twenty-minute fix. Price the support tier into your decision if the site earns you money.

**The regional angle.** If you serve customers in Pakistan from a US-only deployment, every visitor pays a latency tax on every request — and latency is a ranking factor. Cloudflare's edge network is the most forgiving here because it has the widest footprint; on Vercel or Netlify, make sure your functions are deployed in the regions your customers actually live in. And if you bill clients in PKR, remember that every platform charges in dollars — at current rates $20/month is roughly PKR 5,600, and currency moves have quietly raised more Pakistani hosting bills than any platform price change.

**Migration cost.** The most expensive hosting decision is the one you make twice. Moving a production site between platforms — re-testing edge functions, migrating environment variables and secrets, re-pointing DNS, watching for the redirect chains that tank SEO for a week — costs real engineering days. If there's a 60% chance you'll outgrow your pick within a year, pick the one you'd grow into.

## The studio's decision framework

Forget feature matrices. Ask these three questions in order:

**1. What's your traffic shape?**
High-traffic, mostly static → Cloudflare Pages. You're paying for bandwidth you get free there. A content site doing a million page views a month is the textbook case — and one of our [web development cost](/blog/web-development-cost-2026) readers' most common setups.

**2. What's your framework?**
Deep Next.js app with server components, middleware, preview environments per PR → Vercel. The DX dividend outweighs the premium when your team ships daily.

**3. What's your team shape?**
Small team, many small sites, marketing-heavy → Netlify. Flat $20/month with unlimited seats is built for exactly this. Side project or staging → Railway or a Hetzner box with Coolify.

And one hard rule we'd print on a poster: never pick a platform on the free tier alone. Model your bill at 10x your current traffic, then at 10x that. The surprise invoice always arrives the month a post goes viral or an ad campaign works.

## Your checklist before October 23

If you're on a Vercel Pro team, do this before the retention deadline — it takes fifteen minutes:

1. **Open team Settings → Deployment Retention Policy.** Set a longer retention period for *production* deployments if you need rollback targets older than 30 days. Set pre-production, canceled and errored deployments short — preview branches from agent workflows are the storage that Vercel's meter is aimed at.
2. **Check the Usage page** for per-project Deployment Storage and Functions Storage. One large project usually accounts for most of a team's stored output; fix that one first.
3. **Audit add-ons** under Settings → Billing → Add-Ons. Switch off Password Protection, Observability Plus and anything else nobody uses.
4. **Trim image optimisation costs.** Consolidate `deviceSizes` and `imageSizes` to what you actually render, use a single quality value, raise `minimumCacheTTL` for images that never change, and lock down `remotePatterns` so nobody can use your optimiser to resize images from other sites.
5. **Move large assets to Vercel Blob** (or R2, or any object storage) instead of shipping them inside deployments. Big binaries in the repo are the fastest way to feed the storage meter.
6. **Set spend limits.** Day one. Especially if agents deploy on your behalf.

If you do nothing, you'll lose every rollback target older than a month on October 23 and start paying the storage meter at whatever your bill cycle's start date is. For most teams that's fine — deleted history you never rolled back to was dead weight anyway. But make it a decision, not an accident.
