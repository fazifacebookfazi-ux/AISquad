## The question every client asks first

"How much will my website cost?"

We hear it in the first message, almost word for word, from founders in Lahore and retailers in Texas alike. And the honest answer is the one nobody likes: it depends. But "it depends" is not a useful answer when you are trying to budget, so this guide gives you what we give prospective clients — real 2026 price ranges from the Pakistani market, compared with what the same work costs in the United States, and the reasoning behind both.

A quick note on who is writing this: we are a studio based in Pakistan that builds for clients in both countries. We have quoted, built and maintained projects on both sides of this price gap for years. Nothing below is scraped from a directory; these are the numbers we see in real proposals.

## The short answer

If you only want the numbers, here they are. Pakistan prices are what established studios (not the cheapest freelancer on a gig site) charge in 2026. US prices are typical small-to-mid agency rates.

| Website type | Pakistan 2026 | USA 2026 |
|---|---|---|
| Landing page (1–3 sections) | PKR 40k–90k ($150–$320) | $1,500–$4,000 |
| Business website (5–15 pages) | PKR 80k–250k ($280–$900) | $5,000–$15,000 |
| Ecommerce store (Shopify/WooCommerce) | PKR 250k–800k ($900–$2,800) | $10,000–$40,000 |
| Custom web app / SaaS MVP | PKR 600k–2.5M ($2,100–$9,000) | $30,000–$120,000 |
| Marketplace / platform | PKR 1M+ ($3,500+) | $60,000+ |

Two things jump out. First, the gap is roughly **5x to 10x** depending on project type. Second, the gap narrows as complexity rises — senior engineering time is the scarcest resource everywhere, and its price converges faster than design or basic development time.

## Why Pakistan costs less (it is not what you think)

The lazy explanation is "cheap labour," and it is wrong in the way that matters. The engineers building a $900 business site in Lahore are often the same people who would earn $8,000 a month in Dubai or Toronto. The price gap is structural:

**Cost of living.** A senior developer's comfortable life in Lahore costs a fraction of the same life in Austin. Salaries follow living costs, not talent.

**No office theatre.** Most Pakistani studios that serve international clients are remote-first. No downtown lease, no foosball table amortised into your invoice.

**Talent density.** Pakistan produces an enormous number of CS graduates every year, and the freelance platforms trained a generation of them to sell to the West. Competition keeps rates honest.

**Currency math.** When the rupee weakens, dollar-denominated quotes from Pakistani studios get cheaper without anyone taking a pay cut. US clients have quietly benefited from this for years.

None of this means quality is automatically equal. A $150 landing page from a random gig seller and a $1,500 one from a US freelancer differ in process more than in pixels — revisions, testing, communication, and what happens when something breaks. The price gap buys you arbitrage, not a free lunch. Due diligence still matters, which we will come to.

## What actually drives the price

Forget "number of pages" as the main variable. These six factors move quotes more than anything else:

### 1. Custom design vs. template

A template-based site can be excellent — we have shipped template-based work we are proud of. But a template site is assembly; a custom-designed site is architecture. Custom design typically doubles the design portion of a quote, and it is worth it when your brand is the differentiator.

### 2. Content management

A marketing site your team can edit in a CMS (we usually reach for Sanity or a headless WordPress setup) costs more upfront than hardcoded pages, and saves you money every month after. If you will update content weekly, the CMS pays for itself within a quarter.

### 3. Integrations

The site itself is rarely the whole job. Payment gateways (Stripe, JazzCash, Easypaisa for Pakistan), CRMs, booking systems, ERPs — each integration is a small project with its own edge cases. A "simple" site with four integrations is not a simple site.

### 4. Ecommerce complexity

Selling ten products with cash on delivery is one thing. Multi-vendor marketplaces, subscriptions, custom shipping logic, and returns workflows are entirely different animals. Ecommerce quotes scale with operational complexity, not product count.

### 5. Performance and SEO foundations

Core Web Vitals, semantic HTML, meta architecture, sitemaps, structured data — the unglamorous work that decides whether Google ever sends you a visitor. Studios that include it charge more than studios that do not mention it. Guess which one costs less in the long run.

### 6. AI features

The 2026 premium: chatbots that actually resolve queries, search that understands intent, content tools wired into your CMS. AI integration is our home turf, so we will say it plainly — most businesses do not need it yet, and anyone selling it as a default line item is upselling you. When you do need it, budget an extra 20–40% and demand a demo of the exact behaviour before you pay.

## Realistic 2026 price bands, in both currencies

Let us put flesh on the table with what each band actually buys in Pakistan right now.

**PKR 40,000–90,000 — the landing page.** One sharp page: hero, social proof, pricing, FAQ, contact. Copy supplied by you, one revision round, delivered in a week or two. Perfect for validating an idea or supporting an ad campaign.

**PKR 80,000–250,000 — the business website.** This is the sweet spot for most SMEs: 5–15 pages, custom or semi-custom design, CMS, contact/quote forms, WhatsApp integration, basic SEO setup. At the top of this band you get original design and proper performance work.

**PKR 250,000–800,000 — ecommerce.** A real store: product catalogue, cart, checkout with local and international payments, order emails, discount engine, analytics. The range reflects the difference between a clean Shopify setup and a custom build with bespoke workflows.

**PKR 600,000–2,500,000 — custom web apps and SaaS MVPs.** Dashboards, user accounts, subscriptions, APIs. This is engineering, not web design, and it is priced in weeks of senior developer time. Anyone quoting far below this band for a real product is either misunderstanding the scope or planning to disappear halfway.

## The costs nobody puts in the quote

The build price is the headline, not the total. Budget for these from day one:

- **Domain:** PKR 3,000–5,000/year for a .com. Renew it for five years and forget it.
- **Hosting:** $5–$50/month depending on traffic. A static Next.js site on Vercel can cost nearly nothing; a busy WooCommerce store needs real hosting.
- **SSL:** usually free via Let's Encrypt — if a studio charges you for it in 2026, ask why.
- **Premium plugins and SaaS:** page builders, form tools, email services. $20–$100/month adds up; audit it yearly.
- **Content:** photography, copywriting, product images. The number one reason launches slip is not code — it is missing content.
- **Maintenance:** plan 15–20% of the build cost per year. Updates, backups, small changes, and someone to call at midnight when the checkout breaks.

A studio that walks you through this list before you sign is a studio you can trust. One that pretends the quote is the total is selling, not advising.

## Fixed price vs. hourly: our take

We offer both, and we will talk clients out of the wrong one. The rule of thumb:

**Choose fixed price** when the scope is clear — a marketing site, a store with a defined catalogue, a landing page. You know the total before work starts, and the risk of overruns sits with the studio. Insist on milestone-based payments (we do 30/40/30) so incentives stay aligned.

**Choose hourly or weekly** when you are building a product and the requirements will evolve. Fixed-price contracts for evolving products end in change-order warfare; nobody wins.

The red flag is not the pricing model — it is vagueness. "Around PKR 150,000 depending on requirements" without a written scope is not a quote, it is a mood.

## Frequently asked questions

### How much does a basic business website cost in Pakistan in 2026?

A professional 5–10 page business website from a reputable Pakistani studio typically costs **PKR 80,000–250,000** (roughly $280–$900). Freelancers may charge less, but you trade away process, testing and support.

### Why is web development cheaper in Pakistan than in the USA?

The gap is structural, not a quality gap. Lower cost of living, no office overhead for remote-first teams, and a deep talent pool of engineers keep Pakistani rates at roughly **one-fifth to one-tenth** of US agency rates for comparable work.

### What is the average cost of an ecommerce website in Pakistan?

A solid ecommerce build on Shopify, WooCommerce or a custom stack runs **PKR 250,000–800,000** ($900–$2,800) in 2026. Marketplace-style platforms with vendor dashboards start around PKR 1,000,000 and climb with complexity.

### Is it safe to hire a Pakistani agency from the US or UK?

Yes, with the usual due diligence: check live work, insist on a written scope with milestones, and pay in stages. Time-zone overlap with the US East Coast is workable, and most established studios communicate in fluent English.

### What hidden costs should I budget for beyond the build?

Plan for domain renewal, hosting ($5–$50/month), SSL, premium plugins or SaaS tools, content and product photography, and ongoing maintenance — typically **15–20% of the build cost per year**.

### Fixed price or hourly — which is better for a website project?

Fixed price wins when the scope is clear: you know the total before work starts. Hourly suits evolving products where requirements will change. Reputable studios offer both and will tell you which fits your project.
